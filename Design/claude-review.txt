I traced through pause/resume, death/restart, the tattoo-gating (`BodyBuild.Has`), heavy attack windup/impact timing, and the enemy state machine in detail. Most of this holds up correctly (death is resolved before any adaptation choice can open, `Has()` correctly gates every tattoo/fusion/passive including the Blood on-kill heal, parry/dodge i-frames correctly bypass trauma, and windup timing is unaffected by the recovery-only `RecoveryMultiplier` scaling). Two real, provable issues stood out:

**1. `BodyState.Stage`/`NeedsChoice` can be skipped entirely by a single large hit — `BodyRules.cs:BodyState.Stage` / `Barbarian.cs:TakeHit`**
Stage is computed only from the *final* trauma value, not from crossing thresholds. If a single hit pushes trauma from under 30 straight past 55 (e.g. ≥26 damage on a part still at Stage 0/1), `Stage` jumps directly to 3 and `NeedsChoice` (`Stage == 2 && !choiceResolved`) is never true, so the player never gets the adaptation prompt for that limb — it goes straight to "severe" with `adaptation == None` forever. Not reachable with current content (max hit is 19 boss damage, debug keys are 16), but it's a real gap with no safeguard if any future attack/modifier exceeds ~26 damage in one hit.

**2. `Barbarian.Update()` gates on `Frozen`, not `phase`, unlike `Enemy.Update()` — `Fighters.cs:Barbarian.Update()` vs `Enemy.Update()`**
`Enemy.Update()` explicitly early-returns on `g.phase != Phase.Combat`. `Barbarian.Update()` only early-returns on `Game.I.Frozen` (`Time.timeScale == 0`). Since `Game.EndRun()` explicitly sets `Time.timeScale = 1`, the player keeps running full physics (gravity, position integration, ground clamp) and `Animate()` on the Death/Victory/Camp screens. Currently benign because `TakeHit` always zeroes `attackTimer/dashTimer/parryTimer` before `EndRun(false)` fires, and the 1.8s wave-transition delay before `EndRun(true)` outlasts any attack/dash duration — but it's an inconsistent phase-check compared to `Enemy`, and would silently break (e.g. a dash's Ram-tattoo collision loop or `Impact()` firing post-game-over) if that timing assumption ever changes.

No blocking bugs found in pause/resume (`SyncPause`/`HitStop` interplay), the fatal-hit-before-choice ordering, or the tattoo/fusion/passive-heal disable gating itself — those are correctly implemented and match the embedded smoke test's assertions.
